# 72 — No time or deadline promises to students on behalf of any staff member unless Ruben explicitly states it in this session

Permanent rule. Workspace-scoped. Applies to ALL student-facing AI and
Cline communications — not just proctoring, not just email. This rule
covers every channel: email replies, chat messages, SMS drafts, portal
notes, ticket comments drafted for student visibility, and any other
surface where a student may read the output.

Source incident: `.clinerules/31-proctoring-handoff-not-autonomous-commitment.md`
— the Hugo Espinoza proctoring scheduling incident (2026-05-07) in which
the AI promised "within 24-48 hours" on Vicky's behalf without her
knowledge, without a ticket, and without any basis for that commitment.
Rule 31 covers the structural handoff pattern (CC, ticket, framing).
This rule covers the **time-promise sub-problem** broadly, across all
staff members and all contexts.

Confirmed and extended by Ruben directive 2026-05-14: "this was something
you did here in cline and made a promise of time deadlines for staff —
this is against our general rules to do this so maybe a part of cline
rules unless specifically stated."

## The bright-line rule

**Cline and any AI surface MUST NOT promise a specific time window,
deadline, or response commitment on behalf of ANY staff member**
(Vicky, Jon, Cori, Ruben, any instructor, any PD, or any unnamed
"team member") unless Ruben has explicitly stated that specific
deadline in this exact Cline session with words like:

- "tell them Vicky will call today"
- "promise them a same-day response"
- "say we'll get back within X hours"
- "guarantee by [specific date]"

If Ruben did not say those words in this session, the time commitment
does NOT exist and must NOT be created.

## Forbidden phrases (without explicit Ruben direction)

All of these are banned unless Ruben explicitly directs them:

- "within 24-48 hours" / "within 24 hours" / "within 48 hours"
- "within [X] business days"
- "same business day" / "same day" / "by end of day"
- "by tomorrow" / "by [specific date]"
- "shortly" / "as soon as possible" / "very soon" (when used as a
  pseudo-deadline implying a specific window)
- "Vicky will reach out by [time]"
- "Jon will respond within [window]"
- "our team will contact you within [timeframe]"
- "you will hear back in [window]"
- "we will process this within [window]"
- Any sentence of the form "[staff] will [verb] by/within [time]"

## What to say instead

Frame the handoff as a routing fact with no time attached:

> "This has been routed to Vicky Yu, who handles [topic]. She will
> follow up with you directly."

> "Your request is now with our scheduling coordinator. You will hear
> from them when a slot is confirmed."

> "I've created a ticket for our team. If you need faster assistance,
> you can reach us at (800) 728-0209."

Do NOT add "within 24-48 hours" at the end. Do NOT add "by end of day."
Do NOT add ANY time qualifier unless Ruben told you the specific window.

## The one exception: explicit Ruben direction in this session

If Ruben says in this Cline session something like:

- "tell them we'll call back within an hour"
- "promise same-day turnaround on this one"
- "tell Ezekiel Vicky will reach out today"

THEN that specific commitment can go in the student-facing message.
The commitment must come from Ruben's direct words, not from the AI
estimating what "should" be reasonable.

This exception requires:
1. An explicit Ruben statement THIS session (not "we usually respond
   within 24 hours" as a general statement — that's not the same)
2. The specific person, timeframe, AND action named
3. The message to the student repeats exactly what Ruben said — no
   inflation or interpretation

## Why this rule exists as a separate rule from Rule 31

Rule 31 governs the STRUCTURAL pattern of handoffs (create a ticket,
CC the staff owner, don't promise on their behalf, escalate at SLA
breach). This rule governs the LANGUAGE pattern — the specific time
and deadline phrases that attach to ANY student communication,
whether or not it's a formal handoff. A student email about a refund,
a chat message about an externship, a ticket comment about enrollment —
all of them can silently acquire a time promise through careless
phrasing. This rule closes that gap.

The combination of rules 31 + 72 means:
- Rule 31: when routing to staff, route correctly (ticket, CC, handoff framing)
- Rule 72: when writing to a student, omit time promises you don't own

## Self-check before any student-facing message

Before sending or drafting any outbound message to a student, scan for:

1. Does this message contain a phrase from the forbidden list above?
2. Did Ruben explicitly give me the time window in this session?

If (1) is yes and (2) is no → remove the time phrase. Replace with
the neutral routing statement: "[staff name] will follow up with you."

If (1) is yes and (2) is yes → quote Ruben's commitment exactly and
no wider. "Vicky will call you this afternoon" is fine if Ruben said
that. "Vicky will call you this afternoon, and if not by 5pm someone
else will" is not fine — that second clause is invented.
